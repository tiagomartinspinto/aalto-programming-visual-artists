Programming for Visual Artists
Session 04 - Loop Row

Before this sketch existed, drawing eight evenly spaced circles meant
writing circle() eight times by hand:

  circle(25, 50, 30);
  circle(75, 50, 30);
  circle(125, 50, 30);
  circle(175, 50, 30);
  circle(225, 50, 30);
  circle(275, 50, 30);
  circle(325, 50, 30);
  circle(375, 50, 30);

A for loop writes that once and lets a counting variable (i) do the
repeating. Your task is to find that loop and see what each part of it
controls.

---

STEP 1 - Run It

Run the sketch (or open it in the browser). Count the circles.

---

STEP 2 - Find the Loop

Find the for loop in draw(). i starts at 0, the loop keeps running
while i < 8, and i increases by 1 after every repeat - that is why
there are exactly 8 circles.

---

STEP 3 - Find What Changes Each Time

x is calculated from i (25 + i * 50), so every repeat draws its circle
50 pixels further right than the last one. The circle's size (30) does
not depend on i, so every circle is the same size.

---

STEP 4 - Change the Loop

Change 8 to a different number and run it. Then change 50 (the
spacing) to make the circles closer together or further apart.

---

STEP 5 - Make Your Own Row

Change the shape, the color, or make the spacing depend on i as well
(for example, i * i) to make the row uneven on purpose.

---

One line of drawing code, one counting variable, and a loop that
reruns that line as many times as you tell it to - that is the whole
idea. Hover Grid, next, uses two of these loops at once.
