Programming for Visual Artists
Session 06 - Noise Grid

You have been given a grid where every circle's size comes from
noise() instead of random(). Your task is to see what makes noise()
different, then use it to control something of your own.

---

STEP 1 - Run It

Run the sketch (or open it in the browser). Click a few times to see
new compositions.

---

STEP 2 - Compare Nearby Cells

Look at two circles that sit right next to each other. Their sizes are
close, not wildly different. Now compare two circles on opposite
corners of the grid - much less similar. That closeness between
neighbors is what noise() gives you and random() does not:

  random(0, 1)   -> a new, unrelated value every single call
  noise(x, y)    -> nearby (x, y) inputs give nearby output values

---

STEP 3 - Find the noise() Call

Find the line that calculates n using noise(). col and row (multiplied
by noiseScale) are the coordinates it reads from the noise field;
map() then turns that 0-1 value into a pixel size, the same way it
turned mouseX into a color back in Session 02.

---

STEP 4 - Change the Noise Scale

Change noiseScale to 2 and click to regenerate. Nearby cells now look
almost as unrelated as random() would - the noise field is being
sampled at points too far apart to look smooth. Now change it to
0.01: nearly every cell looks the same size, because now the sample
points are too close together to show any change at all. Put it back
to something between 0.1 and 0.3.

---

STEP 5 - Control a Different Property

Instead of using n to set size, use it to set something else - for
example fill(n * 255) for brightness, or the circle's y offset within
its cell.

---

STEP 6 - Create Your Own Organic Grid

Change the grid size, the color, the shape, or combine noise-based
size with a second, independent noise() call for a different property.

---

Two nearby cells look related because they read two nearby points from
the same smooth field - not because anything was copied between them.
That is the whole idea behind noise(): related output from related
input, everywhere else in the field left free to change gradually.
